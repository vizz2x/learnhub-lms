What are Quadratic Equations?

A quadratic equation is a second-degree polynomial equation in a single variable x with a coefficient not equal to zero. The general form is: ax² + bx + c = 0, where a, b, and c are constants and a ≠ 0.

Quadratic equations appear frequently in physics, engineering, economics, and many other fields. Understanding how to solve them is fundamental to advanced mathematics.

💡 Key Insight
The term "quadratic" comes from the Latin word "quadratus" meaning square, because the variable is squared (x²).

Standard Form

Every quadratic equation can be written in standard form:

ax² + bx + c = 0 Where: • a is the coefficient of x² (cannot be 0) • b is the coefficient of x • c is the constant term

Examples

Here are some examples of quadratic equations:

  • x² - 5x + 6 = 0 (where a=1, b=-5, c=6)
  • 2x² + 3x - 2 = 0 (where a=2, b=3, c=-2)
  • x² - 16 = 0 (where a=1, b=0, c=-16)
